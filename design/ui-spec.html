First principles

What makes this world-class — before a single pixel

Flighty's Apple Design Award philosophy is the bar: "We want it to work so well that it feels almost boringly obvious." World-class iOS design in 2026 is not decoration — it is six disciplines applied without exception. Every screen below is judged against these.

1 · Borrow a proven visual language

Flighty borrowed 50 years of airport departure-board conventions. Intelli-Expense borrows the two artifacts its users already trust: the paper receipt (the scan is always visible, always primary) and the finance ledger (tabular numerals, per-currency subtotals, date-grouped rows). Nothing needs explaining because it looks like what it replaces.

Source: Apple "Behind the Design: Flighty" (developer.apple.com/news)
2 · One hero number per screen

Expense apps drown users in numbers. Each screen gets exactly one large, light-weight money figure (the group total, the extracted total); everything else steps down through weight and color, not size. Calm hierarchy is what separates Flighty-class apps from dashboards.

Source: swiftui-design-principles skill — weight-based hierarchy, ≤5 type sizes
3 · Content first, chrome as glass

iOS 26's Liquid Glass exists to "bring focus to the underlying content." So: no custom bar backgrounds, floating system tab bar that minimizes on scroll, scroll-edge effects instead of opaque headers, and glass reserved for the few controls that deserve it — never decorative.

Source: docset /documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass
4 · Uncertainty is a choice, never an error

The extraction pipeline's honesty is the product's soul. When the model is torn, the UI offers two tappable readings with a calm "which is correct?" — no red, no alarm, no modal. The user is the confirmer, not the debugger. This turns the scariest part of AI UX (being wrong) into the most delightful (being asked).

PRD §5.4, §6.3 — ambiguity UX as a first-class requirement
5 · The core loop is sacred: <10 seconds, 0–2 taps of correction

Capture → review → save is the entire app; everything else is furniture. The capture affordance is reachable in one thumb-tap from anywhere, processing shows the receipt (not a spinner), and Save is one prominent action. Speed is a design feature: p50 < 5s is in the PRD as a UX number, not a perf number.

PRD §5.4, §9 — invest UX in the heart of the app
6 · Every state is designed, including the bad ones

Empty states teach the next action. The Apple Intelligence gate explains and self-dismisses. Model-downloading degrades to deterministic extraction with a one-line note. Nothing dead-ends, nothing shows a raw error, and no failure ever loses a captured image.

PRD §5.0, §5.5, §6.4 — designed failure states are must-haves

The rules

  • Dark mode is a token problem, not a layout problem. Nothing moves, resizes or reflows. If a screen needs different layout in dark mode, the light design was wrong.
  • Semantic first: backgrounds, labels, separators, fills and all system glass come from semantic colors and adapt automatically. The custom palette is exactly sixteen token pairs, including twelve category identity hues — every one an asset-catalog color set (Any + Dark), referenced by name in code. colorScheme branches and raw hex are both defects.
  • The receipt image is never dimmed or inverted. The paper is the content and the trust anchor — it renders identically in both appearances, framed by the dark canvas. (The processing screen was designed dark-first for exactly this reason.)
  • Elevation reads through surface, not shadow: true-black grouped canvas, #1C1C1E cards, #2C2C2E tertiary fills. Shadows stay system-default; glass gets its depth from the material, not from heavier borders.
  • Contrast is re-verified in dark, including the amber attention treatments on #1C1C1E and white-on-Ledger-Green in its dark variant. Test at Increased Contrast and with Reduce Transparency, which replaces glass with solid surfaces.

Build it with

  • One asset-catalog color set per custom token (LedgerGreen, CategoryOrange, CategorySlate…), each with Any + Dark appearances; SwiftUI Color("LedgerGreen") / generated symbols. No @Environment(\.colorScheme) in feature code.
  • Semantic colors via SwiftUI defaults (.background, .secondary, list styles) — grouped lists, bars and sheets are already correct.
  • Verify with the Xcode appearance switcher on every screen in design/ui-spec.html; acceptance #10 (fully usable in dark mode) covers this.
Screen group 1

Onboarding & the Apple Intelligence gates

Onboarding does two jobs — say what the app does, get Apple Intelligence on — then gets out of the way. One welcome screen, one conditional gate. Most users never see the gate at all.

Why it's world-class

  • Two screens, zero friction. No feature tour, no permission pre-prompts (camera is requested just-in-time at first scan, per platform convention). The welcome screen's three rows are the mental model of the whole app.
  • The gate treats availability honestly. Blocking only for genuinely blocking states (.appleIntelligenceNotEnabled, .deviceNotEligible); .modelNotReady lets the user in immediately — hard-blocking a person standing at a cash register would lose the receipt.
  • Auto-dismissal is the delight. Turn on Apple Intelligence, flip back, and the gate melts away without a relaunch — the app is watching scenePhase and re-checking SystemLanguageModel.default.availability.
  • Copy discipline: no subtitle duplicates the rows; the middle row introduces on-device intelligence before the gate can appear. Privacy stays in one line, and every button says exactly what it does ("Open Settings", not "Learn more").

Build it with

  • SystemLanguageModel.default.availability branching per PRD §5.0; re-check on scenePhase == .active.
  • Open Settings via public UIApplication.openSettingsURLString only — no private App-prefs: URLs (App Review risk, PRD §5.0).
  • "Welcome seen" in @AppStorage, never in CloudKit.
  • Default currency seeds once from Locale.current.currency into local app storage before the gate check; the welcome row simply makes that choice visible and repairable.
  • Welcome hero uses the generated WelcomeAppIcon imageset, regenerated from AppIcon.icon whenever the icon artwork changes.
Screen group 2

App structure, Folders home & the Add affordance

Three tabs (Folders · Receipts · Settings) is settled by the PRD. Folders home is featured-first: active folders sort pinned first (most recently pinned first), then by activity; the top folder gets one rich card, remaining folders stay compact, Unfiled stays a separate pseudo-section, and completed folders move to a quiet Archived surface instead of being deleted from home.

The home anatomy

  • Featured folder: the top folder in pinned-first order; name, one hero total, metadata, non-interactive breakdown chips (icon + count only — amounts live in folder detail), and up to three real photo thumbnails with hairline borders. Photo-less receipts never render placeholder tiles here. The rail never scrolls; chips wrap onto additional leading-aligned lines at accessibility sizes.
  • Compact rows: name → date range → count in descending weight; the per-currency total right-aligned in tabular numerals with the secondary currency beneath — never summed across currencies (PRD §7).
  • Pinning is quiet and explicit. Long-press a featured or compact active folder for Pin/Unpin. Pinned folders sort above unpinned folders, show one secondary pin glyph, and retain the labeled icon + count category rail; unpinned compact rows stay rail-free. Promotion uses a short ease-in/out transition and never steers capture's activity-based destination.
  • Swipe is Archive, not Delete. Full-swipe on the featured card or compact rows puts the folder away with the same accent-tinted archive treatment as receipts; no dialog, no data loss. Delete lives in Archived Folders and the edit sheet only.
  • Deleting later asks "keep receipts?" → default moves them to Unfiled, which appears as a pseudo-section only when needed.

Why Option A wins

  • It's the honest iOS 26 pattern for a single dominant action. tabViewBottomAccessory(content:) (iOS 26.0+, per the docset) floats the accessory above the tab bar and collapses inline with it when the bar minimizes on scroll — capture stays one thumb-tap away on both browsing tabs without stealing a tab slot or hiding in a corner.
  • It's context-aware. The subtitle shows where the receipt will land ("Berlin — June 2026" when that folder is open, "Unfiled" otherwise) — the folder-defaulting logic of PRD §5.4 made visible before capture, not after.
  • Camera-first, sheet-second. Tapping the bar goes straight to the document camera (the 90% case at a cash register); the labeled More trigger opens the Add Receipt sheet with Scan / Photo / File / Manual and the current destination. Long-press on the bar keeps direct Photo / File / Manual shortcuts for expert use.
  • Honest tradeoff: Apple's canonical accessory is status-like (Music's mini-player). Using it as an action bar is a deliberate, defensible stretch — it's still a system component with system behavior. Hide it on Settings via tab-selection state; if App Review or usability testing pushes back, Option B is a one-day swap.
Recommendation: Option A. The capture bar makes the app's one job physically present on every browsing screen — the "prominent add affordance" of PRD §5 in native iOS 26 material. More and toolbar + share the Add Receipt sheet; long-press keeps the direct secondary shortcuts.
Capture chooser

Add Receipt sheet

More and toolbar + both open the same medium-detent sheet. The sheet shows the destination before the method choice, keeps Scan as the hero, and turns Photo, File, and Manual into explicit rows instead of hiding them behind a system menu.

Interaction contract

  • Accessory tap still scans immediately; accessory More and toolbar + open this sheet.
  • Dismiss with a swipe leaves the app idle. Selecting an option dismisses the sheet first, then starts the same existing scan/photo/file/manual flow.
  • Long-press on the capture accessory keeps direct Photo / File / Manual shortcuts; it is not the primary discovery path.
  • The More trigger keeps the stable identifier and VoiceOver label, with a visible "More" caption in expanded placement and a 44pt-or-larger target.
Profile expansion

Create Folder — profile and categories before save

When the app expands beyond travel, folder creation has to show the receipt shape immediately. The user picks a profile, sees the selected default categories, can add or remove suggestions, and can browse existing categories before creating anything custom — no global category manager, no hidden setup step.

Why this is the right UX

  • It keeps creation honest. A profile is not just a label; it changes what the user will see while saving receipts. Showing Selected categories before Save prevents surprise later.
  • Tap beats drag for add/remove. Apple guidance says drag and drop should have alternate ways to complete the same action. Plus and remove controls are discoverable, accessible, and work with one thumb.
  • Drag still has a job. Long-press reorder inside Selected is useful because ordering affects review and breakdown order, but cross-container dragging is not required to understand the screen.
  • Suggestions are shortcuts, not the catalog. Browse Existing Categories handles "I need something from another profile" without making the main sheet noisy or pushing users into accidental custom duplicates.
  • No unused stuff leaks out. Suggestions are only candidates for this folder. Review, detail, filters, and breakdowns show the selected snapshot plus categories already used by receipts.

Build it with

  • Keep the existing sheet shape: system navigation, grouped form sections, semantic colors, and one Save action.
  • Profile picker is a standard row; changing profile replaces defaults only until the user manually edits categories.
  • Selected category pills/rows are buttons with remove actions; Suggestions are buttons with plus actions; reorder uses standard list edit/reorder affordances at accessibility sizes.
  • Browse Existing Categories opens a searchable sheet of built-in category IDs grouped by suggested, other profiles, and all categories. Custom creation appears only after built-in matches.
  • Hard cap selected categories at nine including Other, and pin Other last in the model so the UI cannot drift.

Design contract

  • App-rendered, so it wears the app. A Home Screen widget is drawn by the app, unlike the system control/tiles above. It follows the app's hierarchy with one scoped exception: Scan is the hero and the only saturated fill, while Photo/File/Manual are soft blue, indigo, and amber-orange chips with matching glyphs.   • No flood, no category semantics, no data. The medium Scan panel is neutral; color lives on chips and the container stays a calm system surface (never a green background, never a gradient).   • Sizes earn their place. Medium (hero Scan + overflow list) and small (2×2 grid) offer the identical four actions. No Lock Screen accessory widget — the scan control already owns the Lock Screen; no large and no data widget.
  • Both lanes are explicit. Durable emits intelliexpense://capture?kind=…; sandbox emits intelliexpense-sandbox://capture?kind=….

Screen group 3

Folder detail — the folder ledger

Where a folder becomes a record: one hero total per currency, the per-type breakdown as slim ledger rows — every type visible at a glance, never a horizontal scroll — then receipts by date. Export lives in the toolbar, exactly where iOS puts share actions.

Why it's world-class

  • The ledger convention: date section headers ("Saturday, June 14"), tabular amounts on a right edge, thumbnails of the actual paper. It reads like the expense report the user will eventually submit — the export is a zero-surprise projection of this screen.
  • One hero number. €1,284.50 in light weight; the second currency deliberately secondaryLabel-small. No pie charts, no progress rings — PRD non-goals kept out.
  • The breakdown is ledger rows, not a rail. One slim full-width row per type — glyph tile, "6 Food" caption, amount right-aligned tabular — in fixed type order inside the hero card. Symmetric for any type count (1–5), everything visible at a glance, and the block previews in miniature the columnar shape of the receipt list below it and the exported CSV. A detail header exists to summarize; content that must be discovered by horizontal scrolling isn't a summary.
  • Breakdown rows are also filters-in-waiting: tapping a row toggles that category's filter (same state as the filter row); the active row fills with its category hue's soft tint.
  • Capture bar context switch: inside a folder, its subtitle names the folder — capture-to-folder without a picker (PRD §5.4 default-folder rule).
  • Filters compose (type × payment) and are visible as chips — state you can see, not a buried filter sheet. Empty-filter result gets its own designed state.

Build it with

  • List + sections keyed by receipt date; header card as a plain section header view (no custom bar backgrounds — glass stays system-owned).
  • Export = ToolbarItem with square.and.arrow.up → §8 zip flow. Toolbar icons only, labeled for VoiceOver (docset toolbars guidance).
  • Totals are computed properties on ExpenseGroup (PRD §4.1), formatted with Decimal.FormatStyle.Currency per receipt currency.
Screen group 4

Capture → Processing — the wait that doesn't feel like waiting

VisionKit's document camera is system UI — we design what happens the instant the shutter closes. The receipt itself is the loading indicator: the user watches their capture being read, never a spinner.

Why it's world-class

  • The image appears instantly — persisted to SwiftData the moment it exists (PRD §9: a crash after this point can never lose the capture). The scan-line treatment says "being read," which is literally true: OCR → parse → Foundation Models.
  • Stage hints are sequential truth, not theater: "Reading text…" (Vision) → "Understanding receipt…" (Foundation Models). If the model times out (~10s) or the language is unsupported, the flow proceeds to review with deterministic values and a one-line note — the user never learns the word "error."
  • Cancel is a promise: it jumps straight to review with whatever exists — even just the image and empty fields. The receipt is captured; nothing else matters.
  • Reduced-motion: scan line becomes a static shimmer; stage text carries the progress story alone.

Build it with

  • VNDocumentCameraViewController wrapped in UIViewControllerRepresentable; multi-page → one ReceiptAttachment per page.
  • Scan line: TimelineView + linear gradient mask, gated on accessibilityReduceMotion.
  • Target p50 <5s: run OCR while the camera sheet dismisses; the processing screen often only lives ~2–3s.
Screen group 5 · The heart · Two options

Review & Confirm — where trust is won

The PRD calls this screen the explicit priority. Both options obey the same laws: image-backed review keeps the paper visible, photo-less manual entry omits that header and starts at Fields, ambiguity is two tappable chips, required fields (type + payment) are structurally unmissable, and Save is the single hero action. They differ in what comes first: the document order or the user's attention.

Laws both options obey

  • Ambiguity = two chips + edit, max. Each chip carries a tiny model-supplied reason ("bottom line SUMME") — provenance in nine characters. Picking one fires .selection haptics and fills the field; more than ~3 ambiguous fields ⇒ fall back to empty editable fields (PRD §5.4's wall-of-choices rule).
  • The image header is evidence, not chrome. It appears only when real page image data exists. A pure manual entry has no placeholder card; its first scrollable content is the Fields section.
  • Date editing is explicit. The date row opens a system sheet with the native graphical calendar. Changes stay staged until Done; Cancel or swipe dismissal preserves the existing receipt date.
  • Attention treatment is a 2.5pt warm edge tint + label, never red walls. Confirmed fields earn small green checkmarks — the screen visibly "completes" as you resolve it.
  • Required ≠ validation nagging. Type and payment are always-visible selectors, not text fields that can fail; Save stays tappable at 60% opacity until required fields are set, with the reason inline above the button when tapped early.
  • Save is glass, floating, singular. Discard is the X, top-leading, and confirms only if the user edited something. Success = .success haptic + the receipt visibly flying into its folder row.

How they differ

  • A (document order) matches the PRD's field order literally; the receipt image is the anchor and every field is a full editor. Predictable, spatial, zero relearning between a clean scan and a messy one.
  • B (attention order) optimizes the cash-register moment: worst case (2 ambiguities) is 2 taps + Save, all above the fold. Cost: field positions change between scans, and "Looks right" rows hide their editors one tap deeper.
Recommendation: Option A for v1 — the PRD's fixed field order is a spec requirement, and spatial stability is what makes review feel "boringly obvious" scan after scan. Fold B's best idea in: when a scan has ambiguities, auto-scroll to the first one and show the "2 fields need a look" count in the header. B remains the A/B-test candidate once real usage shows how often multi-ambiguity scans occur.
Screen group 6

Receipts tab & Receipt detail

The flat, searchable record of everything — and the per-receipt page where the original paper and the extracted record live side by side, forever editable.

Why it's world-class

  • Search lives where iOS 26 wants it — .searchable on this tab, sliding up with the keyboard per the Liquid Glass search conventions. Filters are chips that compose (group ∧ type ∧ payment ∧ has-photo, PRD §7) and each shows an ✕ to remove — state visible, reversible, no hidden filter drawer.
  • Photo-less manual entries don't apologize: the type glyph takes the thumbnail slot, and a prominent Add Photo action on detail runs the pipeline later — extracted values are offered in a diff-confirm, never overwriting what the user typed (PRD §5.2).
  • Provenance without clutter: "View original scan text" is one quiet disclosure over ExtractionRecord.rawOCRText; the footnote states when it was scanned and that extraction was on-device. Trust is shown, not claimed.
  • Editing after saving is the same form as review — one component, one muscle memory, edits update folder totals live (acceptance #8).

Build it with

  • .searchable + #Predicate-backed filters; month sections from Date.FormatStyle().month(.wide).year().
  • Image pager: TabView(.page) over attachments; zoom via full-screen cover with pinch (MagnifyGesture).
  • Row thumbnails from thumbnailData only — 1,000-receipt scroll stays smooth (PRD §9).
Screen group 7

Manual entry & Settings

Manual entry is the review form minus the image: no receipt header, no "Manual entry" placeholder card, and Fields as the first scrollable section. The amount-hero mockup below remains the fuller future manual-entry redesign; Settings is deliberately boring: defaults, status, privacy, version.

Why it's world-class

  • Manual entry respects the moment: the shipped sheet starts directly at the shared review form's Fields section, without spending the first screen on a receipt placeholder. The future focused redesign can promote amount and the decimal pad once that full hierarchy change ships.
  • The sheet is iOS 26 native: inset from the display edges, content peeking beneath, grabber on top — .presentationDetents([.large]) with system glass. No custom chrome to maintain.
  • Settings earns trust by being boring. iCloud state is a green dot and "Up to date", not a sync console; offline shows "Waiting for iCloud — your data is safe on this device" (PRD §6.4). The privacy statement sits exactly where users look for the catch, and there is none.
  • No in-app language picker — locale and CSV decimal style key off Locale.current including per-app language (PRD §3.2-A); currency is seeded once from the device locale, then changed only through the shared picker.
Screen group 8

Export — the payoff moment

The whole app funnels here: folder detail → share icon → a zip your finance team opens without questions. The UI is a progress card that morphs into the system share sheet.

Why it's world-class

  • The filename is the feature: Berlin-June-2026.zip, date folders, 2026-06-14_Lufthansa_EUR-420.00.jpg — a finance admin can audit it without opening the app. Manual entries get a .txt stub so the record is complete (PRD §8).
  • Progress states what it's doing ("11 of 14 · date-named folders") because 200+ receipts must not block the UI (PRD §8) — background task + determinate bar, cancellable.
  • System share sheet, zero custom destinations. AirDrop-to-laptop is the killer path; we don't reinvent it.

Build it with

  • Zip assembly off the main actor; deliver via ShareLink / UIActivityViewController from a temp URL; clean up in defer.
  • CSV: fixed English headers, RFC-4180, UTF-8 BOM; localized comment row only (PRD §3.2-A5).
Screen group 9 · Agent entries · Both platforms

Agent entries — confirmation card

Structured agent entries arrive complete: the agent has already read the receipt, filled every field, and proposed a folder. The app still owns persistence and trust. By default these entries become drafts, shown as one-glance confirmation cards on iPhone and Mac; auto mode saves only when the sidecar attests user confirmation. The full Review & Confirm screen exists to resolve ambiguity, so forcing it on data that is already right would tax the happy path with a screen per receipt. Pending agent entries surface as confirmation cards: the whole record at a glance, one Confirm, and the full Review screen only behind an explicit Review details action. Drafts sync via CloudKit, so a batch entered on the Mac is confirmable from the iPhone: same section, same card, both platforms.

Laws of the confirmation card

  • One card per structured draft. Receipt thumbnail, merchant, right-aligned tabular amount at headline weight, meta line (date · type · payment), assigned folder line, quiet provenance footnote, then Confirm and Review details.
  • Amounts stay at headline weight, tabular. The featured folder card keeps the screen's one hero number; confirmation cards never compete.
  • Confirm is a button, never a swipe or a body tap. Tapping the card body opens Review details. Confirm is the only fast-save action.
  • No confirm-all in v1. Each confirm is one deliberate tap with the full record in sight; a five-receipt batch is five taps.
  • Provenance is a footnote, not a warning. "Entered by agent" is secondary-label quiet; no warning tint or model-trust visual language.
  • Zero chrome when empty. The section exists only while pending entries exist, same law as the Archived row.
  • Same component on both platforms. iPhone placement is above the Folders featured card; Mac placement is at the top of the content column above receipt date sections, at Mac density with Confirm as the default-button equivalent.
  • Raw bridge drops are different. Raw or sidecar-less files run the normal pipeline and appear on Mac as pending review through the existing review screen, not this structured card.

Build it with

  • Use the shared thumbnail, formatter, type name, payment name, and review-form components. Add no new colors, badges, or model-trust visual language.
  • VoiceOver reads the card as one record with Confirm and Review Details as actions; "Entered by agent" is read as the provenance line.
